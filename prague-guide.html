Express Summary

The 60-second version — jump to any section below for full detail and sources.

Top 5 sights

  1. Charles Bridge (go at sunrise)
  2. Prague Castle complex + St. Vitus Cathedral
  3. Old Town Square & Astronomical Clock
  4. Letná Park (Prague 7 — Metronome view)
  5. Old Jewish Cemetery, Josefov

Top 5 eats

  1. Lokál Dlouhááá — classic Czech, reserve ahead
  2. Kantyna — Czech meats, best burger in town
  3. Holešovice Market (Prague 7) — Skô, Naše maso
  4. Field or La Degustation — Michelin, special occasion
  5. Aromi (Vinohrady) — best Italian in Prague

Do this, skip that

  • Do: spend a half/full day in Prague 7 (Holešovice/Letná) — real local scene, not just a photo stop
  • Do: Kutná Hora as a half-day trip (~1 hr each way)
  • Consider skipping: a Český Krumlov day-trip in Jul/Aug — stay overnight or go shoulder season
  • Book ahead: Lokál, and any Michelin pick

Logistics, fast

  • Buy transit tickets in the PID Lítačka app
  • Tip ~10%, rounded up
  • Watch bags on Charles Bridge / Old Town Square
  • Currency: CZK — cards widely accepted

Classic Sights — Old Town, Castle & Josefov

The core loop most guides (Lonely Planet included) build a Prague trip around.

Landmark

Old Town Square & Astronomical Clock

15th-century astronomical clock chimes on the hour with a small mechanical show. Anchor point for Old Town.

Landmark

Charles Bridge

650-year-old Gothic bridge lined with 30 baroque statues. Go at sunrise to beat the crowds.

Landmark

Prague Castle Complex

One of the largest castle complexes in the world — St. Vitus Cathedral, Golden Lane, Old Royal Palace. Grounds are free; paid circuits cover the interiors.

History

Josefov — Old Jewish Cemetery

Haunting historic Jewish Quarter with the Old Jewish Cemetery and several synagogues.

Off the main loop

Vyšehrad Fortress

Quieter hilltop fortress and park south of the center — river views without Castle-district crowds.

Free

Lesser Town (Malá Strana)

Cobbled lanes below the Castle; Lennon Wall and Kampa Island are easy add-ons on the walk up.

Prague 7 — Holešovice & Letná

The district you specifically asked about: riverside, less touristy, and a genuine local food/art scene.

Culture & parks

Letná Park (Letenské sady)

Hilltop park with sweeping views over Charles Bridge and the river; home to the giant Metronome, on the site of the former Stalin monument.

Veletržní palác (Trade Fair Palace)

National Gallery's home for 19th–21st century and modern/contemporary art.

DOX Centre for Contemporary Art

Leading contemporary art space with rotating exhibitions and the "Gulliver" airship installation.

Výstaviště Praha

Historic exhibition grounds; events, markets, and the illuminated Křižík Fountain shows.

Stromovka

Prague's largest park, mostly frequented by locals rather than tourists.

Bio Oko

Independent cinema and active cultural venue with a great bar, hosts events and festivals.

Where to eat

Holešovice Market

Central food hub of the district. New arrivals as of late 2025 include Skô (Slovak-influenced, slow-roasted & wood-fired by chef Tomáš Valkovič) and Naše maso Holešovice, the nose-to-tail butcher's outpost from the beloved Old Town original.

The Eatery

Modern Czech cuisine.

SaSaZu

Southeast Asian flavors, also a nightlife venue.

Mr. HotDoG

Gourmet hot dogs — casual pick.

Bar Cobra

Blends café culture by day with nightlife by night.

Coffee

21 Coffee

Specialty coffee, inviting atmosphere.

Café Letka

Gourmet coffee, shabby-chic interior.

Format Espresso Bar

Minimalist, focused on high-quality specialty coffee.

Nightlife

Cross Club

Distinctive industrial/steampunk-style club — a Prague institution for alternative nightlife.

Letná beer garden (Nad Letenským sadem)

Outdoor beer garden in the park with sunset views over the river.

Suggested route: Vltavská metro (line C) → Veletržní palác → DOX → Holešovice Market (lunch) → Výstaviště → up into Letná Park for the Metronome view and a beer at the garden → walk down the Letná stairs toward Malá Strana.

Food & Drink — City-wide

Classic Czech

Lokál Dlouhááá

Dlouhá 33, Staré Město. Goulash, schnitzel, fried cheese, and tank-fresh Pilsner Urquell in a long, 1970s-style hall. Reserve ahead — it fills up fast.

U Fleků

Historic brewery/beer hall dating back centuries, known for its own dark lager.

Kantyna

Local Czech meats, great beer, and what's often called the best burger in Prague.

U Kroka / Tiskárna

Upscale-feeling traditional Czech cuisine, not too pricey.

Hidden gems & local favorites (Time Out picks)

Etapa

Karlín — curated brunch/lunch menu, fresh and local.

KRO Karlin Bistro & Cocktail Bar

Chicken-focused menu, casual charm.

Gao Den

Prague's best Vietnamese, unassuming location — the fried spring rolls stand out.

The Spot Cafe

Hidden gem in a historic Old Town-adjacent neighborhood.

Vinohrady & Karlín — local, low-tourist dining

If Prague 7 whets your appetite for non-touristy neighborhoods, these two are the other local favorites.

Café Sladkovský

Vinohrady institution on Sevastopolská — Czech-modern cooking, seasonal ingredients.

Aromi

One of Prague's best Italian restaurants — fresh pasta, natural wines.

Vinohradský Parliament

Craft beer bar and kitchen on Korunní Street, rotating Czech taps.

Riegrovy sady

Popular sunset park with beer gardens — full of locals with drinks and picnics in summer.

Karlín (general)

Rebuilt after the 2002 floods into one of Prague's leading food & culture districts — chic restaurants, cafés, wine bars (e.g. Veltlin).

Day Trips

~1 hr by train

Kutná Hora

UNESCO-listed medieval silver-mining town. The main draw is the Sedlec Ossuary ("Bone Church"), decorated with the bones of roughly 40,000 people, plus the Late Gothic St. Barbara's Cathedral. Round-trip train ~140–180 CZK. Easily doable as a half/full day.

~2.5–3 hrs by train/bus

Český Krumlov

Fairy-tale medieval town encircled by the Vltava, topped by a castle. Local experts recommend staying overnight rather than rushing a day trip — the town empties out and feels completely different once the day-trip coaches leave. If doing it in a day, avoid July/August; shoulder season (Mar–May, Sep–Nov) is much better.

Practical Info

Transport: Buy tickets via the PID Lítačka app or station machines. A 24h or 72h pass covers metro, trams and buses — validate paper tickets on boarding.
Tipping: ~10%, usually rounded up.
Safety: Very safe overall; watch for pickpockets on Charles Bridge, Old Town Square, and crowded trams/metro.
