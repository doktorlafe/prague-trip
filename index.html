Prague, Beyond the Postcard

A field-researched guide drawing on Lonely Planet, Time Out, Fodor's, the MICHELIN Guide, Prague City Tourism and the city's local press

The castle and the bridge still earn every superlative thrown at them. But the real story is Prague 7, the river district of former slaughterhouses, galleries and beer gardens where the city's chefs, artists and night owls actually spend their time. Here is the full briefing.

The Briefing

For readers short on time, here is the verdict up front. Full reporting, with sourcing, follows below.

Top 5 sights

  1. Prague Castle and St. Vitus Cathedral (arrive at 9:00)
  2. Charles Bridge at sunrise
  3. Old Town Square and the Astronomical Clock
  4. Josefov, the Jewish Quarter
  5. Letná Park, for the best view in town

Top 5 eats

  1. Lokál Dlouhááá for classic Czech food and tank beer
  2. Holešovice Market: Skô, Naše maso
  3. Kantyna, a butcher-restaurant
  4. Field or La Degustation (MICHELIN stars)
  5. Café Sladkovský in Vinohrady

Do this

  • Give Prague 7 a full day
  • Kutná Hora as a half-day trip
  • Buy transit tickets in the app, which is cheaper than paper
  • Book Lokál and any MICHELIN table ahead

Skip that

  • Street exchange offices and Euronet ATMs
  • Taxis hailed on the street; use Bolt or Liftago
  • Restaurants on Old Town Square itself
  • A Český Krumlov day trip in July or August

Before You Go

The practical ground rules. Most of these trip up first-time visitors at least once.

Money

Czech koruna, not euro

Cards and contactless work almost everywhere. When a terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency, decline and pay in CZK, because the "conversion" typically adds a 3–7% markup.

Tipping

Around 10%

Round up or add about 10% for good table service. Tell the server the total you want to pay when they bring the card machine or take cash.

When to go

Shoulder season wins

April–May and September–October bring mild weather and thinner crowds. July and August are hot and packed. December has the Christmas markets and cold, short days.

Language

Czech

English is widely spoken in central Prague and by younger people. A "dobrý den" (good day) on entering a shop or pub goes a long way; see the phrasebook.

Power

Type E/F sockets, 230 V

Standard continental European two-pin plugs. UK, US and other visitors need an adapter.

Water

Tap water is safe

Prague tap water is drinkable. In restaurants, tap water isn't automatically served, so ask for it explicitly ("kohoutková voda").

How it works: Buy in the PID Lítačka app, which is cheaper than paper, or at yellow machines and newsagents. Prague in Five Minutes

You'll understand what you're looking at far better with the eleven centuries behind it in mind.

  • c. 880 Prince Bořivoj founds Prague Castle on the hill above the Vltava.
  • 1348 Emperor Charles IV founds Charles University, the oldest in Central Europe, and New Town. Prague becomes an imperial capital.
  • 1357 Construction begins on the stone bridge later named after Charles IV.
  • 1410 The Astronomical Clock is installed on the Old Town Hall.
  • 1618 The Second Defenestration of Prague, when imperial officials were thrown from a Castle window, helps ignite the Thirty Years' War.
  • 1918 Czechoslovakia is founded, with Prague as its capital.
  • 1939–45 Nazi occupation. Most of Prague's Jewish community is deported, many via Terezín.
  • 1948 The Communist coup. Seven years later a giant Stalin monument goes up on Letná hill; it is demolished in 1962.
  • 1968 The Prague Spring reforms are crushed by a Warsaw Pact invasion.
  • 1989 The Velvet Revolution ends Communist rule; mass rallies fill Letná Plain.
  • 1991 The giant Metronome is installed where Stalin once stood.
  • 1993 Czechoslovakia splits peacefully; Prague becomes capital of the Czech Republic.
  • 2004 The Czech Republic joins the European Union.

The Real Story: Prague 7

Ask a Prague local where they actually eat and drink, and the answer is rarely Old Town. Prague 7 (Holešovice, Letná and Troja) packs a riverside of former slaughterhouses and factories, the city's best park views, its biggest galleries, and the zoo into one district most first-time visitors walk straight past.

Letná: the view and the beer garden

Letná Park (Letenské sady)

Hilltop park with the definitive postcard view down the Vltava's bridges. The giant red Metronome stands where the Stalin monument did until 1962.

Letná beer garden

Picnic tables under chestnut trees, plastic cups, cheap beer and a sunset over the bridges. This is where Prague actually drinks on summer evenings.

National Technical Museum

Vintage cars, locomotives and aircraft under one roof, beside Letná Park. It's great with kids and with engineers.

Holešovice: markets, galleries, stages

Pražská (Holešovická) tržnice

Art Nouveau and Neo-Renaissance halls that served as the city slaughterhouse until the 1980s. Hall 22 is Prague's largest produce market: Czech farm cheese, sausages, pastries, mushrooms and flowers.

Veletržní palác (Trade Fair Palace)

The National Gallery's modern and contemporary collection, in a vast 1920s functionalist building.

DOX Centre for Contemporary Art

The leading contemporary art space in the city. Don't miss "Gulliver", a 42-metre wooden airship perched on the roof.

Jatka78

A former slaughterhouse hall turned home of new circus and progressive theatre, including Cirk La Putyka. Book a show for an evening Old Town can't offer.

Vnitroblock

A warehouse turned café, restaurant and event space in the middle of a residential block between Tusarova and Dělnická streets.

Bio Oko

Beloved independent cinema with a good bar and a busy festival programme.

Výstaviště Praha

Historic exhibition grounds, home to the Křižík Fountain light shows and a rotating calendar of fairs.

Stromovka & Troja: green Prague 7

Stromovka

A former royal game park and now Prague's largest park, used almost entirely by locals. Cross it on foot toward Troja.

Planetarium Prague

On the edge of Stromovka, with a 22-metre dome recently converted to LED projection. Open Tue–Fri 8:30–20:30, weekends 10:30–20:30.

Prague Zoo (Troja)

Opened in 1931, it now holds around 670 species in a dramatic hillside setting and is regularly ranked among the world's best zoos. Plan half a day.

Where to eat and drink in Prague 7

Skô

At Holešovice Market since late 2025: chef Tomáš Valkovič's Slovak-rooted, wood-fired and slow-roasted cooking.

Naše maso Holešovice

The market outpost of Prague's cult nose-to-tail butcher. Order the burger or the meatloaf and eat standing.

Trang An

Highly rated Vietnamese inside the market complex, a nod to Prague's large Vietnamese community.

SaSaZu

Glamorous Southeast Asian fusion in the market complex, which doubles as a club venue.

The Eatery

Modern Czech cooking in a clean, contemporary room.

Coffee: 21 Coffee · Café Letka · Format

Specialty coffee three ways: cosy (21), shabby-chic (Letka), minimalist espresso bar (Format).

Bar Cobra

A café by day and a cocktail bar by night, and a Letná favourite.

Cross Club

A steampunk labyrinth of salvaged metal and moving machinery; the alternative club night Prague is known for.

The Prague 7 walk (about 5 km): Metro C to Vltavská → Holešovice Market, Hall 22 (breakfast) → Veletržní palác → lunch at Skô or Naše maso → DOX → Výstaviště → through Stromovka → back up to Letná Park for the Metronome view and sunset at the beer garden → down the Letná steps to the river and Old Town.

Beer, decoded

Hladinka

The standard pour: beer with a firm, creamy head of about three fingers.

Šnyt

Mostly foam with a little beer, a smaller "one more before I go".

Mlíko

"Milk": a glass of pure, sweet foam, drunk in one go. Try it once.

Desítka / Dvanáctka

"Ten" and "twelve" refer to the degree scale of the brew. A desítka is lighter and a dvanáctka is fuller-bodied; Pilsner Urquell is a twelve.

The classics

Lokál Dlouhááá

Dlouhá 33, Old Town. Goulash, schnitzel and fried cheese under a very long 1970s-style ceiling, with Pilsner Urquell poured from tanks. Reservations are essential in the evening.

U Fleků

A brewery and beer hall that has brewed its own dark lager on site for centuries. It's touristy, but historic.

Kantyna

A butcher's counter turned restaurant: Czech meats, great beer, and a burger often called the best in Prague.

U Kroka / Tiskárna

Traditional Czech cooking with an upscale feel, without the upscale prices.

Local favourites (Time Out picks)

Etapa

Karlín. A short, curated brunch and lunch menu built on local produce.

KRO Karlín

A bistro and cocktail bar where chicken is the star; get the quarter chicken with baked potatoes.

Gao Den

Unassuming room, Prague's best Vietnamese. Order the fried spring rolls.

The Spot Cafe

A true hidden gem in the historic centre.

Two More Neighbourhoods Worth the Detour

If Prague 7 sold you on skipping the tourist trail, Vinohrady and Karlín are where that instinct pays off again, with chef-driven menus, craft beer and very few tour groups.

Vinohrady

Café Sladkovský

A neighbourhood institution on Sevastopolská serving seasonal, modern Czech cooking.

Karlín

Out of Town: Four Assignments

Choose by how much time you have, and how much patience for tour buses.

~1 h by train · half day

Kutná Hora

A UNESCO-listed medieval silver-mining town. The Sedlec Ossuary is decorated with the bones of some 40,000 people, arranged into a chandelier and a coat of arms. St. Barbara's Cathedral is Late Gothic at its most ambitious.

Trains leave from Praha hlavní nádraží; a return costs about 140–180 CZK.

~40 min by train · half day

Karlštejn Castle

Charles IV's Gothic castle, built to guard the crown jewels. It rises dramatically above a village 30 km southwest of Prague.

Trains run from hlavní nádraží or Smíchovské nádraží every 30–60 minutes, then it's a 20–30 minute uphill walk. Interiors are visited on guided tours only.

~1 h · sobering half day

Terezín Memorial

The fortress town the Nazis turned into a ghetto and transit camp. Many of Prague's Jews passed through it on the way to the death camps. Give it proper time.

Buses leave from Nádraží Holešovice, in Prague 7.

~2.5–3 h · overnight advised

Český Krumlov

A fairy-tale town wrapped in a bend of the Vltava beneath a vast castle. Local experts say to stay overnight: the town transforms once the day-trip coaches leave.

Avoid July and August. Go from late March to May or from September to November.

Traps, Scams & How to Dodge Them

Prague is very safe, and the old taxi-mafia days are largely over. Its remaining traps are about money, and all of them are avoidable.

#1 complaint

Exchange offices

Shops near Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square advertise "0% commission" and build the margin into a dreadful rate. Never change money with people on the street.

ATMs

Euronet machines

These standalone non-bank machines charge heavy fees and push poor conversion rates. Use bank ATMs such as Česká spořitelna, ČSOB or Komerční banka, and always choose CZK.

Taxis

Street and rank cabs

These are rare now, but still found at the main station and at airport parking. Use Bolt or Liftago, which charge about 300–450 CZK from the airport, against 1,000+ CZK from a rogue cab.

Restaurants

Old Town Square pricing

Some venues on and around the square run a separate, inflated tourist menu. Check prices before ordering and read the bill. Walking two streets away usually halves the cost.

Pickpockets

Crowd hotspots

Charles Bridge, the Astronomical Clock crowd, and the tourist-packed tram 22. Keep phones in front pockets.
